\begin{abstract}
    The goal of RNA design is to find, within an exponentially large sequence space, a nucleotide sequence that folds into a specified 
    target structure. While essential for developing RNA-based therapeutics such as riboswitches and ribozymes, efficiently identifying 
    sequences with high target folding probability remains challenging, especially for long sequences. Currently, the state-of-the-art method, 
    SamplingDesign, suffers from extreme computational inefficiency. To address this bottleneck, we present EliteDesign, a novel framework 
    that leverages the cross-entropy method to iteratively refine a distribution over candidate RNA sequences. On the Eterna100 benchmark,
     EliteDesign achieves a higher average target folding probability than SamplingDesign while running $500\times$ faster. Furthermore, 
     EliteDesign scales efficiently to long RNA structures far beyond the reach of SamplingDesign: on ten 16S rRNA targets (950–1,525 nucleotides), 
     it achieves an average target folding probability of 0.51 versus 0.20 for the strongest scalable baseline, SAMFEO, while accelerating runtime
      by $24\times$.
\end{abstract}
