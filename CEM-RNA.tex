\begin{abstract}
    The aim of RNA Design is to find, within an exponentially large search space,
    a nucleotide sequence that folds into a given target structure. Efficient RNA design could aid in
    the development of RNA-based technologies such as riboswitches and ribozymes. However, 
    finding sequences that fold into the target structure with a high probability remains challenging, 
    particularly for long RNAs. To address this challenge, we introduce an approach that uses the 
    Cross-Entropy method to iteratively refine a distribution over valid RNA sequences. Our method 
    outperforms other state-of-the-art methods, achieving an average folding probability of over 0.60 
    on Eterna100, with a runtime of seven minutes per seed. It also scales to longer RNA structures, reaching 
    an average folding probability of 0.51 on ten long 16S rRNA targets, compared with 0.20 
    for SAMFEO, while recording a sevenfold lower average runtime per puzzle.
\end{abstract}
