\section{\textcolor{titleblue}{Conclusion}}
In conclusion, this work introduces EliteDesign, an RNA design framework that uses the
cross-entropy method to find high-probability RNA sequences for a given target structure.
We further show that this method addresses a gap in current RNA design
methods by enabling the design of sequences longer than 1,000 nucleotides with high
target folding probability. Our method beats the state-of-the-art SamplingDesign on Eterna100 in both
speed and quality. Additionally, we show large gains over SAMFEO on ten 16S
rRNA targets while remaining much faster per puzzle. We also show that our
method achieves higher target probability on other RNA design datasets such as Rfam~\citep{kalvari2021}
and RNAsolo764~\citep{adamczyk2022}. Despite these gains, EliteDesign has some limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item EliteDesign uses the same initialization strategy for every target, which may not work
  equally well for all structures. During optimization, it can concentrate too early on
  a narrow set of sequences and miss better solutions.
\item EliteDesign uses LinearPartition to estimate folding probabilities during search. Errors in these estimates
  can cause it to favor weaker candidates and miss better ones.
  Exact scoring at the end checks the final candidates but cannot recover sequences
  missed during search.
\end{enumerate}
Future work could add a local refinement stage similar to SAMFEO~\citep{zhou2023},
which might further improve NED and target probability on some structures.
