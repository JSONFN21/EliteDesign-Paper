The goal of RNA design is to find, within an exponentially large sequence
space, a nucleotide sequence that folds into a specified target structure.
While essential for developing functional RNAs such as riboswitches and ribozymes,
efficiently identifying sequences with high Boltzmann probability remains challenging,
especially for long sequences. Currently, the state-of-the-art method, SamplingDesign, is computationally very expensive,
because its gradient-based optimization takes many small steps and scores thousands of sequences at each step.
To address this bottleneck, we present EliteDesign, a novel framework that leverages the
gradient-free cross-entropy method to iteratively refine a distribution over candidate RNA sequences.
On the Eterna100 benchmark, EliteDesign achieves a higher average Boltzmann probability than
SamplingDesign while running 178$\times$ faster. Furthermore, EliteDesign scales efficiently to long RNA structures
far beyond the reach of SamplingDesign. On ten 16S rRNA targets (950--1,525 nucleotides),
it achieves an average Boltzmann probability of 0.52 versus 0.20 for the
strongest scalable baseline, SAMFEO, while accelerating runtime by 14$\times$.
